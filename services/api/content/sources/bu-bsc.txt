# bsc-fs-1-forensic-science-intro | 1 | Forensic Science: Principles and Criminology
## Introduction to Forensic Science
### History, principles and branches
= Definition, history, basic principles and branches of forensic science.
- Forensic science applies scientific methods to questions of law.
- Locard's exchange principle: every contact leaves a trace.
- Other principles: individuality, progressive change, comparison, analysis, probability.
- Pioneers: Bertillon (anthropometry), Galton and Henry (fingerprints), Locard (first police crime laboratory, Lyon, 1910).
- Branches: biology, serology, DNA, chemistry, toxicology, physics, ballistics, documents, fingerprints, digital forensics.
> State and apply Locard's exchange principle
> Name the branches of forensic science and their work
hook: A burglar wears gloves and wipes the door handle. What might he still leave behind, and take away?
q: Who established the first police crime laboratory? || Edmond Locard, in Lyon in 1910.
q: What does the principle of individuality say? || Every object, natural or man-made, has an individuality that is not duplicated in another object.
terms: Locard's principle; trace evidence; individuality
## Forensic Science Organisations
### Forensic laboratories in India
= Organisation of forensic science services in India.
- Central Forensic Science Laboratories (CFSLs) work under the Directorate of Forensic Science Services (DFSS), Ministry of Home Affairs.
- Each state has a State FSL with Regional and District mobile units.
- Other agencies: Central Fingerprint Bureau (NCRB), Government Examiners of Questioned Documents, National Forensic Sciences University (Gandhinagar).
- BNSS section 176(3) requires a forensic expert to visit the crime scene for offences punishable with seven years or more.
> Describe the structure of forensic services in India
> Explain the mandatory forensic visit under BNSS
## Criminology
### Crime and its causes
= Concepts of crime, schools of criminology and theories of causation.
- Crime is an act or omission punishable by law; criminology studies crime, criminals and society's response.
- Schools: classical (Beccaria, free will and deterrence), positivist (Lombroso, born criminal), sociological.
- Theories: differential association (Sutherland), strain (Merton), labelling, routine activity.
> Compare schools of criminology
> Apply theories to explain a crime
## Forensic Science and the Law
### Expert evidence
= Admissibility of scientific evidence and the role of the expert witness.
- BSA section 39 (formerly Evidence Act section 45) makes the opinion of experts relevant.
- Reports of certain government scientific experts can be used as evidence without calling them (BNSS section 329).
- An expert must be qualified, impartial, and explain the basis of the opinion; the court decides the weight.
- Chain of custody documents every transfer of evidence.
> Explain the role and duties of an expert witness
> Maintain chain-of-custody records

- First responder: ensure safety, give aid, secure and cordon the scene, record entry and exit.

### Packaging and preservation
= Collecting, packaging and preserving physical and biological evidence.
- Biological evidence is air-dried and packed in paper (not plastic) to prevent mould.
- Volatile samples (arson debris) go in airtight metal cans.

## Fingerprints
### Fingerprint patterns and classification
= Ridge characteristics, pattern types and the Henry system.
- Fingerprints are unique and permanent from fetal life.
- Pattern types: arches (about 5%), loops (60–65%), whorls (30–35%).
- A loop has one delta; a whorl two; a plain arch none.
- Henry classification assigns values to whorls in finger pairs to form a primary classification.

- Cyanoacrylate fuming develops prints on non-porous items; iodine fuming is non-destructive and temporary.

## Questioned Documents
### Handwriting and signature examination
= Principles of handwriting identification and forgery.
- No two people write alike, and no one writes exactly the same way twice (natural variation).
- Class characteristics (taught system) vs individual characteristics (habits).
- Forgery types: simulated, traced, freehand; signs include tremor, pen lifts, blunt starts and patching.
- Admitted and specimen writings are standards for comparison.
> Compare questioned and standard writings
> Identify signs of forgery
### Inks, alterations and paper
= Detecting alterations, erasures and ink differences.
- Alterations show under UV, IR and oblique light; VSC (video spectral comparator) is the standard instrument.
- Inks are compared by thin-layer chromatography.
- Indentations are revealed by ESDA (electrostatic detection apparatus).
> Detect alterations with light sources
> Compare inks by chromatography

## Forensic Physics
### Glass and soil evidence
= Physical properties used to compare glass and soil.
- Glass is compared by density and refractive index (immersion method, Becke line).
- Radial cracks form on the side opposite the impact; concentric cracks on the same side (3R rule: radial cracks form right angles on the reverse side).
- Soil is compared by colour, particle size and mineral content.

### Tool marks and impressions
= Footwear, tyre and tool mark evidence.
- Impressions may be 2-D (dust, blood) or 3-D (mud, snow); 3-D impressions are cast with dental stone.
- Class characteristics (sole pattern, size) narrow the source; wear and damage individualise it.
- Stature can be estimated from foot length using population-specific regression formulae.

## Forensic Ballistics
### Firearms and ballistics
= Firearm classification, internal, external and terminal ballistics.
- Rifled arms leave land and groove marks on bullets; smooth-bore (shotguns) do not.
- Internal ballistics: inside the barrel; external: in flight; terminal: on the target.
- Entry wounds are smaller with abrasion collars; exit wounds are larger and irregular.
- Range of fire is estimated from soot and tattooing (powder stippling).

## Toxicology
### Poisons and their analysis
= Classification of poisons, specimens and extraction.
- Paracelsus: "the dose makes the poison".
- Classes: corrosives, irritants (metallic: arsenic, mercury, lead), neurotoxins, asphyxiants (CO, cyanide).
- Viscera (stomach, intestine, liver, kidney), blood and urine are preserved; saturated salt solution is used for viscera (not in cases of corrosive acid poisoning).
- Stas–Otto method extracts alkaloids and organic poisons.

### Arson and explosives
= Fire chemistry, accelerants and explosive residues.
- Fire triangle: fuel, heat, oxygen (tetrahedron adds the chain reaction).
- Accelerants (petrol, kerosene) are recovered from debris by headspace sampling and identified by GC.
- Low explosives deflagrate (black powder); high explosives detonate (TNT, RDX, PETN).

- Rf = distance moved by the spot ÷ distance moved by the solvent front.
- GC separates volatile compounds; HPLC non-volatile ones; MS identifies compounds by mass-to-charge ratio.
- IR spectroscopy identifies functional groups; UV-Vis follows Beer–Lambert's law A = εcl.

## Death and Post-mortem Changes
### Signs of death and time since death
= Death, early and late post-mortem changes.
- Early changes: algor mortis (cooling, roughly 1 °C per hour early on), livor mortis (hypostasis, fixed in about 6–8 hours), rigor mortis (begins 2–6 hours, full by about 12, passes by 24–36 hours).
- Late changes: decomposition, adipocere, mummification.
- Entomology (insect succession) helps estimate time since death in later stages.
> Estimate time since death from post-mortem changes
> Explain factors affecting cooling and rigor
## Injuries
### Mechanical injuries
= Abrasions, contusions, lacerations, incised and stab wounds.
- Abrasion: loss of superficial skin; contusion: bruise from ruptured vessels; laceration: tear from blunt force.
- Incised wounds are longer than deep with clean edges; stab wounds are deeper than long.
- Defence wounds on hands and forearms suggest the victim resisted.
> Classify mechanical injuries
> Distinguish suicidal, homicidal and accidental wounds
## Asphyxia
### Asphyxial deaths
= Hanging, strangulation, drowning and smothering.
- Hanging: ligature mark oblique, above the thyroid, incomplete; ligature strangulation: horizontal, below the thyroid, complete.
- Drowning signs: froth at mouth and nostrils, water in the stomach, diatoms in bone marrow.
- Petechial haemorrhages are seen in many asphyxial deaths.
> Distinguish hanging and strangulation
> List signs of drowning
## Forensic Anthropology and Odontology
### Identification from skeletal and dental remains
= Estimating sex, age and stature from bones and teeth.
- Sex: pelvis (wider sub-pubic angle in females) and skull features.
- Age: epiphyseal fusion and ossification centres in the young; dental eruption in children.
- Stature from long bones (e.g. femur) using regression formulae.
- Bite marks and dental records aid identification.

= SN1, SN2, E1 and E2 mechanisms.

## Spectroscopy
### UV-Visible and IR spectroscopy
= Electronic transitions, Beer–Lambert law and IR functional group frequencies.
- Beer–Lambert law: A = εcl.
- UV-Vis transitions: σ→σ*, n→σ*, π→π*, n→π*; conjugation shifts λmax to longer wavelength.
- IR: O–H about 3200–3600 cm⁻¹ (broad), C=O about 1700 cm⁻¹, C≡N about 2250 cm⁻¹.

### NMR and mass spectrometry
= Chemical shift, splitting and molecular ion.
- ¹H NMR chemical shift (ppm from TMS) depends on electronic environment; aldehyde H ≈ 9–10 ppm.
- n + 1 rule: a proton with n equivalent neighbours splits into n + 1 peaks.
- In MS, the molecular ion peak gives the molar mass; the base peak is the most intense.

## Electrochemistry
### Conductance
= Conductivity, molar conductivity and Kohlrausch's law.
- Molar conductivity Λm = κ × 1000 ÷ C.
- Strong electrolytes: Λm = Λ°m − A√C; weak electrolytes: degree of dissociation α = Λm ÷ Λ°m.
- Kohlrausch's law: Λ°m = ν₊λ°₊ + ν₋λ°₋.

### Electrochemical cells
= EMF, Nernst equation and pH measurement.
- E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode.
- Nernst equation: E = E° − (0.0591/n) log Q at 298 K.
- ΔG° = −nFE°.
- pH is measured with a glass electrode; pH-metric titration finds the equivalence point from the steepest rise.

### Polymerisation and polymers

### Industrial processes and pollution
= Fertilisers, cement, water treatment and pollution.
