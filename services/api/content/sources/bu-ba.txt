# ba-jour-1-communication | 1 | Journalism: Introduction to Communication
## Communication Concepts
### Nature and process of communication
= Definitions, elements and types of communication.
- Elements: source, message, channel, receiver, feedback, noise.
- Types: intrapersonal, interpersonal, group, public and mass communication.
- The 7 Cs: clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete, courteous.
> Describe the communication process
> Distinguish types of communication
## Models of Communication
### Communication models
= Linear, interactive and transactional models.
- Lasswell: who says what, in which channel, to whom, with what effect.
- Shannon–Weaver: information source, transmitter, channel (with noise), receiver, destination.
- Schramm's model adds a shared field of experience; Osgood–Schramm is circular.
> Compare communication models
> Apply Lasswell's model to a news story
## Mass Communication
### Functions and theories of mass media
= Functions of media and early effects theories.
- Functions (Lasswell, Wright): surveillance, correlation, transmission of culture, entertainment.
- Hypodermic needle (magic bullet) theory assumed powerful direct effects; two-step flow (Lazarsfeld) found opinion leaders mediate.
- Agenda setting (McCombs and Shaw): media tell people what to think about.
> Explain functions of mass media
> Compare media effects theories
## Media in India
### Indian media landscape
= Print, broadcast and digital media in India.
- Print is regulated through the Press and Registration of Periodicals Act, 2023 (replacing the PRB Act, 1867).
- Prasar Bharati runs Doordarshan and All India Radio (Akashvani).
- Digital news grows through mobile phones and regional languages.
> Describe India's media landscape
> Identify regulators of each medium

# ba-jour-2-reporting | 2 | Journalism: Reporting
## News
### Concept of news and news values
= What makes news and the elements of a news story.
- News values: timeliness, proximity, prominence, impact, conflict, human interest, novelty.
- The 5 Ws and 1 H: who, what, when, where, why, how.
- Inverted pyramid: most important facts first.
> Identify news values in events
> Write a lead with the 5 Ws and 1 H
hook: A minister falls ill; a farmer in your district finds a 3 kg mango. Which is front-page news, and why?
ex: Lead: "Bengaluru recorded 120 mm of rain on Monday, the city's heaviest single-day October rainfall in a decade, flooding underpasses in five wards."
q: What does the inverted pyramid put first? || The most important information.
terms: lead; news values; inverted pyramid; beat
## Reporting Techniques
### Sources and interviews
= News sources, interviewing and verification.
- Sources: primary (eyewitnesses, documents) and secondary; on record, off record, background.
- Interview types: news, personality, investigative; prepare, listen, follow up.
- Verify facts with at least two independent sources.
> Conduct and write up an interview
> Verify information
## Beats
### Reporting beats
= Crime, courts, politics, business, sports and development reporting.
- Beat reporters cultivate sources in one area.
- Court reporting must avoid contempt and identify victims of sexual offences only as the law permits (they must not be named).
- Development journalism reports on social and economic progress.
> Report on a beat accurately
> Follow legal limits in crime and court reporting
## Writing for News
### News writing styles
= Hard news, features and the structure of stories.
- Hard news is timely and factual; features add depth, colour and human interest.
- Attribution: say who said it; quotes must be accurate.
- Headlines are short, active and accurate.
> Write hard news and feature stories
> Write accurate headlines

# ba-jour-3-editing-media-law | 3 | Journalism: Editing and Media Laws | year=2025-26
## Editing
### Copy editing and headlines
= Role of the copy editor, editing symbols and headline writing.
- Copy editors check accuracy, clarity, style, grammar and legal risks.
- Style books keep usage consistent.
- Page layout uses grids, hierarchy and white space.
> Edit copy for accuracy and style
> Design a news page layout
## Press Freedom
### Constitutional framework
= Article 19(1)(a) and reasonable restrictions.
- Freedom of the press is part of freedom of speech and expression under Article 19(1)(a) (Romesh Thappar, 1950).
- Article 19(2) allows reasonable restrictions: sovereignty, security, public order, decency, contempt of court, defamation, incitement.
- The Press Council of India (Press Council Act, 1978) adjudicates complaints against and by the press.
> Explain press freedom and its limits
> Describe the Press Council's role
## Media Laws
### Defamation, contempt and other laws
= Laws journalists must know.
- Defamation is a civil wrong and a crime (BNS section 356); truth for public good and fair comment are defences.
- Contempt of Courts Act, 1971; Official Secrets Act, 1923; Right to Information Act, 2005.
- Copyright Act, 1957 protects original works; fair dealing allows reporting current events.
> Avoid defamation and contempt
> Use RTI for reporting
## Ethics
### Journalism ethics
= Codes of conduct, privacy and fake news.
- PCI Norms of Journalistic Conduct: accuracy, fairness, right of reply, privacy.
- Paid news and conflicts of interest undermine credibility.
- Fact-checking counters misinformation and deepfakes.
> Apply ethical codes to cases
> Fact-check a viral claim

## Introduction
### Psychology as a science
= Definition, schools and methods of psychology.
- Psychology is the scientific study of behaviour and mental processes.
- Schools: structuralism (Wundt, first lab 1879), functionalism (James), psychoanalysis (Freud), behaviourism (Watson, Skinner), humanistic, cognitive.
- Methods: experiment, observation, survey, case study, correlational.

> Choose a research method
## Biological Bases
### Nervous system and behaviour
= Neurons, the nervous system and the brain.
- Neurons transmit signals by action potentials and neurotransmitters across synapses.
- CNS: brain and spinal cord; PNS: somatic and autonomic (sympathetic, parasympathetic).
- Brain lobes: frontal (planning, movement), parietal (touch), temporal (hearing, memory), occipital (vision).

# ba-psy-2-cognitive | 2 | Psychology: Learning, Memory and Cognition
## Learning
### Classical and operant conditioning
= Pavlov's and Skinner's learning principles.
- Classical conditioning: a neutral stimulus paired with an unconditioned stimulus comes to elicit a conditioned response.
- Operant conditioning: reinforcement increases behaviour; punishment decreases it.
- Observational learning (Bandura's Bobo doll study).
> Distinguish classical and operant conditioning
> Apply reinforcement principles
hook: Why does your mouth water when you hear a packet of chips being opened?
q: What is negative reinforcement? || Increasing a behaviour by removing an unpleasant stimulus.
q: In Pavlov's experiment, what was the conditioned stimulus? || The bell (or metronome).
terms: conditioning; reinforcement; extinction; generalisation
## Memory
### Models of memory and forgetting
= Atkinson–Shiffrin model, working memory and forgetting.
- Sensory memory → short-term memory (about 7 ± 2 items, around 20 seconds) → long-term memory.
- Baddeley's working memory: central executive, phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, episodic buffer.
- Forgetting: decay, interference (proactive, retroactive), retrieval failure; Ebbinghaus forgetting curve.
> Explain memory models
> Apply strategies to improve memory
## Thinking
### Problem solving and decision making
= Concepts, reasoning, heuristics and biases.
- Algorithms guarantee solutions; heuristics are shortcuts.
- Biases: availability, representativeness, anchoring, confirmation bias.
- Functional fixedness and mental set block problem solving.
> Identify cognitive biases
> Use problem-solving strategies
## Intelligence
### Theories and measurement of intelligence
= Theories of intelligence and IQ.
- Spearman's g; Thurstone's primary abilities; Gardner's multiple intelligences; Sternberg's triarchic theory.
- IQ = (Mental age ÷ Chronological age) × 100 (ratio IQ); modern tests use deviation IQ (mean 100, SD 15).
- Emotional intelligence includes perceiving, using, understanding and managing emotions.
> Compare theories of intelligence
> Compute a ratio IQ

## Cognitive Development
### Piaget and Vygotsky
= Stages of cognitive development and sociocultural theory.
- Piaget: sensorimotor (0–2), preoperational (2–7), concrete operational (7–11), formal operational (11+).
- Key concepts: schema, assimilation, accommodation, object permanence, conservation.
- Vygotsky: zone of proximal development and scaffolding.

## Socio-emotional Development
### Erikson and attachment
= Psychosocial stages and attachment styles.
- Erikson's eight stages, e.g. trust vs mistrust (infancy), identity vs role confusion (adolescence).
- Attachment (Bowlby, Ainsworth's strange situation): secure, avoidant, ambivalent, disorganised.
- Parenting styles (Baumrind): authoritative, authoritarian, permissive, neglectful.

> Classify attachment styles

### Later development
= Adolescence, adulthood and ageing.
- Puberty brings physical and hormonal change; identity formation (Marcia's statuses).
- Moral development (Kohlberg): pre-conventional, conventional, post-conventional.
- Ageing: crystallised intelligence stays stable; fluid intelligence declines.

# ba-psy-4-social | 4 | Psychology: Social Psychology | year=2025-26
## Social Cognition
### Attribution and social perception
= How we explain behaviour and form impressions.
- Fundamental attribution error: overemphasising personality in others' behaviour.
- Self-serving bias: credit success to self, failure to circumstances.
- Primacy effect in impression formation.
> Identify attribution errors
> Explain impression formation
## Attitudes
### Attitudes and persuasion
= Attitude formation, change and cognitive dissonance.
- Cognitive dissonance (Festinger): inconsistency motivates change in attitudes or behaviour.
- Elaboration likelihood model: central route (arguments) vs peripheral route (cues).
- Prejudice is an attitude; discrimination is behaviour.
> Explain cognitive dissonance
> Apply the elaboration likelihood model
## Social Influence
### Conformity, compliance and obedience
= Classic studies of social influence.
- Asch: people conform to a group's wrong judgement.
- Milgram: about 65% obeyed to the highest shock level.
- Compliance techniques: foot-in-the-door, door-in-the-face.
> Describe classic social influence studies
> Recognise compliance techniques
## Group Processes
### Groups and pro-social behaviour
= Social facilitation, loafing, groupthink and helping.
- Social facilitation improves simple tasks in the presence of others; social loafing reduces individual effort in groups.
- Bystander effect: more bystanders, less likelihood of help (diffusion of responsibility).
- Group polarisation strengthens initial views.
> Explain group effects on performance
> Explain the bystander effect

### Defining abnormality and classification
= Criteria of abnormality and DSM-5-TR and ICD-11.
- Criteria: statistical rarity, deviance, distress, dysfunction, danger.
- Classification systems: DSM-5-TR (APA) and ICD-11 (WHO).

## Anxiety and Mood Disorders
### Anxiety and depression
= Symptoms and causes of anxiety and mood disorders.
- Anxiety disorders: generalised anxiety, panic, phobias, social anxiety; OCD is classified separately in DSM-5.
- Major depressive disorder: depressed mood or loss of interest for at least two weeks with other symptoms.
- Bipolar disorder involves manic or hypomanic episodes.

## Psychotic and Other Disorders
### Schizophrenia and personality disorders
= Psychotic disorders and personality disorders.
- Schizophrenia: positive symptoms (delusions, hallucinations) and negative symptoms (flat affect, avolition).
- Personality disorders are grouped in clusters A (odd), B (dramatic), C (anxious).
- Neurodevelopmental disorders include ADHD and autism spectrum disorder.

= Rights of persons with mental illness in India.
