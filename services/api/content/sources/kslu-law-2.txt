# llb-4-public-international-law | 4 | Public International Law
## Nature and Sources
### Nature and sources of international law
= Whether international law is law, and its sources.
- Sources (Article 38(1), ICJ Statute): treaties, custom, general principles of law; judicial decisions and teachings as subsidiary means.
- Custom needs state practice and opinio juris (North Sea Continental Shelf, 1969).
- Jus cogens norms (e.g. prohibition of genocide, torture) cannot be derogated from.
> Identify sources of international law
> Explain the elements of custom
## States and Recognition
### Statehood, recognition and jurisdiction
= Criteria of statehood, recognition theories and state jurisdiction.
- Montevideo Convention criteria: population, territory, government, capacity to enter relations.
- Recognition: constitutive and declaratory theories; de jure and de facto.
- Jurisdiction principles: territorial, nationality, protective, universal.
> Apply criteria of statehood
> Distinguish theories of recognition
## Treaties and the Sea
### Law of treaties and law of the sea
= Vienna Convention, 1969 and UNCLOS, 1982.
- Pacta sunt servanda (Article 26, VCLT); reservations; rebus sic stantibus (fundamental change of circumstances).
- UNCLOS zones: territorial sea up to 12 nautical miles, contiguous zone 24, exclusive economic zone 200.
- Innocent passage is allowed through the territorial sea.
> Apply treaty rules
> Identify maritime zones and rights
## International Institutions and Disputes
### The United Nations and the ICJ
= UN organs, peaceful settlement and use of force.
- Principal UN organs: General Assembly, Security Council, ECOSOC, Trusteeship Council, ICJ, Secretariat.
- Article 2(4) of the UN Charter prohibits the use of force; exceptions are self-defence (Article 51) and Security Council action (Chapter VII).
- ICJ jurisdiction rests on consent (special agreement, compromissory clause, optional clause).
> Explain the prohibition on the use of force
> Describe how the ICJ gets jurisdiction

# llb-4-company-law | 4 | Company Law
## Corporate Personality
### Incorporation and lifting the veil
= Separate legal personality and when courts pierce it.
- A company is a separate legal person (Salomon v Salomon, 1897).
- The veil is lifted for fraud, sham companies, tax evasion and enemy character; section 7(7) and others allow statutory lifting.
- Kinds: private, public, OPC, small, Section 8 (charitable), producer companies.
> Explain corporate personality
> Identify when the veil is lifted
## Constitution and Capital
### MOA, AOA and share capital
= Constitutional documents, doctrines and capital.
- Ultra vires acts are void (Ashbury Railway Carriage v Riche, 1875).
- Indoor management (Royal British Bank v Turquand) protects outsiders; constructive notice applies to public documents.
- Share capital: equity and preference; buy-back (section 68); reduction of capital (section 66).
> Apply ultra vires and indoor management
> Explain capital alteration
## Management and Meetings
### Directors, meetings and governance
= Directors' duties, meetings and minority protection.
- Directors' duties (section 166): act in good faith, with due care, avoid conflicts.
- Oppression and mismanagement remedies (sections 241–244) before the NCLT; class actions (section 245).
- Related party transactions (section 188) need approval.
> State directors' duties
> Advise minority shareholders
## Insolvency and Winding Up
### Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016
= Corporate insolvency resolution and liquidation.
- CIRP starts on default of at least ₹1 crore; financial or operational creditors may apply to the NCLT.
- A resolution professional runs the company; the committee of creditors approves a resolution plan by 66% vote.
- Timeline: 180 days, extendable by 90, with an outer limit of 330 days.
> Describe the CIRP
> Explain the waterfall in liquidation (section 53)

# llb-4-evidence-bsa | 4 | Law of Evidence (Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023)
! Section numbers are of the BSA; Indian Evidence Act equivalents may still be expected.
## Relevancy
### Facts in issue and relevant facts
= Relevancy, res gestae and motive.
- Evidence may be given only of facts in issue and relevant facts.
- Res gestae: facts forming part of the same transaction are relevant (section 4).
- Motive, preparation and conduct are relevant (section 6).
> Distinguish facts in issue and relevant facts
> Apply res gestae
## Admissions and Confessions
### Admissions, confessions and dying declarations
= When statements are admissible against their makers.
- A confession to a police officer is not provable (section 23); a confession under inducement, threat or promise is irrelevant (section 22).
- Information leading to the discovery of a fact is admissible (proviso to section 23, formerly section 27).
- A dying declaration as to the cause of death is relevant (section 26(a)); it can alone support conviction if reliable.
> Decide admissibility of confessions
> Evaluate a dying declaration
hook: An accused tells police, "I threw the knife in the well." The knife is found there. What can the court hear?
q: Is a confession made to a police officer admissible? || No (section 23), except the part that leads to discovery of a fact.
terms: admission; confession; discovery; dying declaration
## Documentary and Electronic Evidence
### Documents and electronic records
= Primary and secondary evidence and electronic records.
- Primary evidence is the document itself; secondary evidence is admissible in listed cases.
- Electronic records are documents and can be primary evidence (sections 57, 61).
- A certificate under section 63 (formerly 65B; Arjun Panditrao Khotkar, 2020) is needed for electronic records as secondary evidence.
> Prove documents and electronic records
> Prepare a section 63 certificate
## Burden of Proof and Witnesses
### Burden, presumptions and examination of witnesses
= Who must prove, presumptions, and examination-in-chief and cross-examination.
- Burden of proof lies on the person who asserts (section 104).
- Presumptions: "may presume", "shall presume", "conclusive proof".
- Leading questions are not allowed in examination-in-chief but are allowed in cross-examination.
> Allocate the burden of proof
> Conduct examination and cross-examination

# llb-4-ipr | 4 | Intellectual Property Rights Law
## Introduction
### Nature of IP and international framework
= Kinds of IP and TRIPS.
- IP rights: copyright, patents, trademarks, designs, geographical indications, trade secrets.
- TRIPS Agreement (WTO) sets minimum standards.
- IP rights are territorial and time-limited (except trademarks, renewable).
> Classify IP rights
> Explain TRIPS obligations
## Patents
### Patents Act, 1970
= Patentability, exclusions and term.
- Patentable invention: novelty, inventive step, industrial application.
- Section 3(d) bars new forms of known substances without enhanced efficacy (Novartis v Union of India, 2013).
- Term: 20 years from filing; compulsory licences (section 84).
> Test patentability
> Explain section 3(d)
## Copyright
### Copyright Act, 1957
= Works, rights, term and fair dealing.
- Protects original literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works, films and sound recordings.
- Term: author's life plus 60 years for literary works.
- Fair dealing for private use, research, criticism and reporting (section 52).
> Identify protected works
> Apply fair dealing
## Trademarks and Designs
### Trade Marks Act, 1999 and Designs Act, 2000
= Registration, infringement and passing off.
- A trademark must be capable of distinguishing goods or services; registration lasts 10 years, renewable.
- Infringement for registered marks; passing off protects unregistered goodwill.
- Design registration lasts 10 years, extendable by 5.
> Assess trademark infringement
> Distinguish infringement and passing off

# llb-5-cpc-limitation | 5 | Civil Procedure Code and Limitation Act
## Suits and Jurisdiction
### Jurisdiction and bars to suits
= Civil jurisdiction, res sub judice and res judicata.
- Civil courts try all suits of a civil nature unless barred (section 9).
- Res sub judice (section 10) stays a later suit on the same matter; res judicata (section 11) bars re-litigation of decided matters.
- Place of suing (sections 15–20): the lowest competent court; where the defendant resides or the cause of action arises.
> Decide which court has jurisdiction
> Apply res judicata
## Pleadings and Trial
### Plaint, written statement and interim orders
= Pleadings, appearance and interim relief.
- Plaint (Order VII); rejection of plaint (Order VII Rule 11).
- Written statement within 30 days, extendable to 90 days (120 days, non-extendable, in commercial suits).
- Temporary injunctions (Order XXXIX Rules 1–2) need a prima facie case, balance of convenience and irreparable injury.
> Draft the structure of a plaint
> Apply the test for temporary injunction
## Judgment, Appeal and Execution
### Decree, appeal, review, revision and execution
= Post-trial remedies.
- Decree (section 2(2)); first appeal (section 96); second appeal on a substantial question of law (section 100).
- Review (section 114, Order XLVII); revision (section 115); inherent powers (section 151).
- Execution of decrees by the court that passed it or to which it is sent (section 38).
> Choose the right post-decree remedy
> Explain execution procedure
## Limitation
### Limitation Act, 1963
= Bar of limitation, extension and computation.
- A suit filed after the limitation period is dismissed even if limitation is not pleaded (section 3).
- Condonation of delay for appeals and applications on sufficient cause (section 5); not for suits.
- Common periods: suit on contract 3 years; residuary 3 years (Article 113); possession of immovable property based on title 12 years.
> Compute limitation periods
> Apply section 5 condonation

# llb-5-criminal-procedure-bnss | 5 | Criminal Procedure (Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023)
! Section numbers are of the BNSS; CrPC equivalents may still be expected.
## Pre-trial Process
### FIR, investigation and arrest
= Information to police, investigation and arrest powers.
- FIR for cognizable offences (section 173); e-FIR and zero FIR are recognised.
- Preliminary enquiry is allowed for offences punishable with 3 to 7 years (section 173(3)).
- Arrest without warrant (section 35); notice of appearance instead of arrest in suitable cases; arrest memo and informing relatives (D. K. Basu guidelines).
> Explain registration of FIR
> State safeguards on arrest
## Custody and Bail
### Remand and bail
= Police and judicial custody, default bail and anticipatory bail.
- Police custody up to 15 days in total, which may be taken in parts within the first 40 or 60 days (section 187).
- Default bail if the charge-sheet is not filed within 60 or 90 days.
- Bailable and non-bailable offences; anticipatory bail (section 482); "bail is the rule, jail the exception".
> Compute remand and default bail periods
> Advise on anticipatory bail
## Trial
### Charges and trials
= Charge, trial procedures and plea bargaining.
- Trials: sessions, warrant, summons and summary trials.
- Plea bargaining (Chapter XXIII) is available for offences punishable up to 7 years, except specified offences.
- Trial in absentia of proclaimed offenders is now possible in specified cases.
> Identify the trial procedure for an offence
> Explain plea bargaining
## Appeals and Other Provisions
### Appeals, revision and maintenance
= Post-trial remedies and miscellaneous proceedings.
- Appeals against conviction and acquittal; revision by Sessions Courts and High Courts.
- Maintenance of wives, children and parents (section 144).
- Victim compensation scheme (section 396).
> Choose post-trial remedies
> Advise on maintenance and victim compensation

# llb-5-interpretation | 5 | Interpretation of Statutes
## Rules of Interpretation
### Literal, golden and mischief rules
= Primary rules of statutory interpretation.
- Literal rule: plain meaning of words.
- Golden rule: modify literal meaning to avoid absurdity.
- Mischief rule (Heydon's case, 1584): find the mischief the law was meant to remedy; purposive interpretation is its modern form.
> Apply the three rules to a statute
> Choose an approach for an ambiguous provision
## Subsidiary Rules
### Maxims of interpretation
= Ejusdem generis, noscitur a sociis and others.
- Ejusdem generis: general words following specific ones take their colour.
- Noscitur a sociis: a word is known by its companions.
- Expressio unius est exclusio alterius: express mention of one excludes others.
> Apply interpretive maxims
> Interpret general words in a list
## Aids to Interpretation
### Internal and external aids
= Preamble, headings, definitions and legislative history.
- Internal aids: title, preamble, headings, definitions, provisos, explanations, schedules.
- External aids: parliamentary debates, committee reports, dictionaries, earlier statutes.
- General Clauses Act, 1897 supplies common definitions and rules.
> Use internal and external aids
> Apply the General Clauses Act
## Interpretation of Special Statutes
### Penal, taxing and beneficial statutes
= Strict and liberal construction.
- Penal and taxing statutes are construed strictly in favour of the subject.
- Beneficial and welfare legislation is construed liberally.
- Presumption of constitutionality; reading down to save a statute.
> Construe penal and taxing statutes
> Apply the presumption of constitutionality

# llb-5-professional-ethics | 5 | Professional Ethics and Accounting for Lawyers (Clinical)
## The Legal Profession
### Advocates Act, 1961
= Enrolment, Bar Councils and rights of advocates.
- State Bar Councils enrol advocates; the Bar Council of India sets standards and the All India Bar Examination.
- Only enrolled advocates may practise in courts (section 30).
- Misconduct leads to disciplinary proceedings (section 35).
> Describe the regulation of advocates
> Identify professional misconduct
## Duties of Advocates
### BCI Rules on professional conduct
= Duties to court, client, opponent and colleagues.
- Duties to the court: act with dignity, not influence the court by illegal means.
- Duties to the client: not withdraw without sufficient cause, keep confidences, avoid conflicts.
- Advertising and solicitation are prohibited (Rule 36).
> Apply the BCI Rules to cases
> Explain the bar on advertising
## Contempt and Bar–Bench Relations
### Contempt of court
= Civil and criminal contempt and defences.
- Contempt of Courts Act, 1971: civil (wilful disobedience) and criminal (scandalising, prejudicing proceedings).
- Truth is a defence if in the public interest and bona fide (2006 amendment).
- Mutual respect between bar and bench sustains justice.
> Distinguish civil and criminal contempt
> Identify defences to contempt
## Accounts for Lawyers
### Accountancy for lawyers
= Client money, books of account and fees.
- Advocates must keep accounts of clients' money separately.
- Fees cannot be contingent on the result (no share in the subject matter).
- Receipts and statements of account must be given to clients.
> Maintain client accounts
> Explain rules on fees

# llb-6-adr | 6 | Alternative Dispute Resolution (Clinical)
## ADR Overview
### Forms of ADR
= Arbitration, conciliation, mediation, negotiation and Lok Adalats.
- Section 89 CPC lets courts refer disputes to ADR.
- Negotiation and mediation are non-binding until settled; arbitration gives a binding award.
- ADR is quicker, cheaper and confidential.
> Choose an ADR method
> Explain section 89 CPC
## Arbitration
### Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996
= Arbitration agreement, tribunal, award and challenge.
- An arbitration agreement must be in writing (section 7); courts refer parties to arbitration (section 8).
- Appointment of arbitrators (section 11); the tribunal rules on its own jurisdiction (section 16, kompetenz-kompetenz).
- Award within 12 months of completion of pleadings, extendable by 6 months (section 29A); set aside under section 34; enforced under section 36.
> Draft an arbitration clause
> State grounds to set aside an award
## Mediation and Conciliation
### Mediation Act, 2023
= Pre-litigation mediation and mediated settlement agreements.
- Mediation is voluntary; a mediated settlement agreement is final and enforceable like a court judgment.
- Mediation should be completed within 120 days, extendable by 60.
- Online mediation is recognised.
> Explain the mediation process
> Describe enforcement of settlements
## Lok Adalats and Legal Aid
### Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987
= Lok Adalats and free legal aid.
- A Lok Adalat award is deemed a civil court decree and final; no appeal lies.
- Permanent Lok Adalats handle public utility services disputes.
- Free legal aid is a right under Article 39A and Article 21 (Hussainara Khatoon).
> Explain Lok Adalat procedure
> Identify who is entitled to legal aid

### Human rights: concept and generations
= Nature, generations and the international bill of rights.
- Human rights are universal, inalienable, indivisible and interdependent.
- Generations: civil and political; economic, social and cultural; collective (development, environment).

= Charter-based and treaty-based bodies.

## Human Rights in India
### Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993
= NHRC, SHRCs and human rights courts.
- The NHRC inquires into violations and recommends action; its chair is a former Chief Justice of India or Supreme Court judge.
- The NHRC cannot inquire into matters more than one year old.
- Human rights courts are designated sessions courts.

- SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989; Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016.
