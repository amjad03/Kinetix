# bba-1-management-principles | 1 | Management Principles and Practice
## Introduction to Management
### Management: concept, functions and skills
= Meaning and nature of management, managerial roles and skills.
- Management is a process of planning, organising, staffing, directing and controlling to achieve goals efficiently and effectively.
- Efficiency is doing things right (minimum resources); effectiveness is doing the right things (achieving goals).
- Mintzberg's managerial roles: interpersonal, informational, decisional.
- Katz's skills: technical (lower levels), human (all levels), conceptual (top level).
> Explain the functions and roles of a manager
> Relate managerial skills to levels of management
## Evolution of Management Thought
### Classical, behavioural and modern approaches
= Scientific management, administrative theory, bureaucracy, Hawthorne studies and systems and contingency approaches.
- Taylor's scientific management: science not rule of thumb, harmony, cooperation, maximum output, development of workers.
- Fayol's 14 principles include division of work, authority and responsibility, unity of command, unity of direction, scalar chain and equity.
- Max Weber's bureaucracy: hierarchy, rules, impersonality, merit-based appointment.
- The Hawthorne studies (Elton Mayo) showed social factors affect productivity; systems and contingency approaches see the organisation as open and its best practice as situational.
> Compare classical and behavioural approaches
> Apply Fayol's principles to a case
## Planning and Decision Making
### Planning, objectives and decisions
= Types of plans, steps in planning, MBO and decision-making models.
- Strategic plans are long-term and set by top management; tactical plans by middle managers; operational plans for day-to-day work.
- SMART objectives are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound.
- Programmed decisions handle routine problems; non-programmed decisions handle new, unstructured ones.
- Simon's bounded rationality: managers "satisfice" rather than optimise.
> Write SMART objectives
> Distinguish programmed and non-programmed decisions
## Organising
### Structure, authority and delegation
= Organisation design, centralisation and delegation.
- Departmentation by function, product, territory, customer or process.
- A narrow span of control creates a tall structure; a wide span a flat one.
- Centralisation keeps decision authority at the top; decentralisation spreads it systematically.
- Authority can be delegated but accountability cannot.
> Design a structure for a growing firm
> Explain the elements of delegation
## Directing and Controlling
### Communication, coordination and control
= Communication process and barriers, coordination and the control process.
- Communication process: sender, encoding, message, channel, decoding, receiver, feedback.
- Barriers: semantic, psychological, organisational and physical.
- Coordination is the essence of management: it synchronises group efforts.
- Control process: set standards, measure, compare, correct; feedforward control acts before problems occur.
> Identify communication barriers and remedies
> Describe the control process

# bba-1-business-accounting | 1 | Fundamentals of Business Accounting
## Introduction to Accounting
### Accounting principles and the accounting equation
= Meaning, users and branches of accounting, and the accounting equation.
- Users: owners, managers, creditors, investors, employees, government.
- Branches: financial, cost, management and tax accounting.
- Accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Capital; every transaction keeps it balanced.
- Golden rules: personal — debit the receiver, credit the giver; real — debit what comes in, credit what goes out; nominal — debit expenses and losses, credit incomes and gains.
> Apply the accounting equation to transactions
> Classify accounts and apply the golden rules
hook: You start a business with ₹50,000 and buy goods for ₹20,000 on credit. What do you own and what do you owe?
ex: Capital ₹50,000: Assets (cash) 50,000 = Capital 50,000.
ex: Goods ₹20,000 on credit: Assets 70,000 = Liabilities 20,000 + Capital 50,000.
q: Which account is debited when rent is paid? || Rent account (nominal: debit expenses).
terms: asset; liability; capital; debit; credit
## Journal, Ledger and Trial Balance
### Recording and posting
= Journal entries, subsidiary books, ledger posting and the trial balance.
- The journal is the book of original entry; entries are posted to ledger accounts and balanced.
- Subsidiary books: purchases, sales, returns, cash book, bills books and the journal proper.
- A trial balance lists ledger balances; equal totals show arithmetical accuracy but not the absence of all errors.
- Errors not disclosed by a trial balance: omission, commission (some), principle and compensating errors.
> Journalise transactions and post them to the ledger
> Prepare a trial balance
## Bank Reconciliation Statement
### Bank reconciliation
= Reasons for differences between cash book and pass book and the reconciliation statement.
- Causes: cheques issued but not presented, cheques deposited but not cleared, bank charges, interest and direct credits or debits not yet in the cash book.
- Starting from the cash book (debit) balance: add cheques issued but not presented; deduct cheques deposited but not cleared.
- An overdraft is a credit balance in the cash book.
> Prepare a bank reconciliation statement
> Explain the causes of difference
ex: Cash book balance ₹25,000; cheques issued not presented ₹4,000; deposited not cleared ₹6,000; bank charges ₹200.
ex: Pass book balance = 25,000 + 4,000 − 6,000 − 200 = ₹22,800.
## Final Accounts of Sole Traders
### Trading and profit and loss account and balance sheet
= Preparing final accounts with adjustments.
- Gross profit = Net sales − Cost of goods sold (Opening stock + Purchases + Direct expenses − Closing stock).
- Common adjustments: closing stock, outstanding and prepaid expenses, accrued and advance income, depreciation, bad debts and provision for doubtful debts.
- Each adjustment is recorded twice: once in the trading or profit and loss account and once in the balance sheet.
> Prepare final accounts with adjustments
> Treat outstanding and prepaid items correctly
## Depreciation
### Methods of depreciation
= Causes of depreciation and the straight-line and written-down value methods.
- Depreciation is the allocation of the depreciable amount of an asset over its useful life.
- Straight-line: annual depreciation = (Cost − Residual value) ÷ Useful life.
- Written-down value: a fixed rate on the opening book value; the charge falls each year.
- Profit or loss on sale = Sale price − Book value on the date of sale.
> Compute depreciation by both methods
> Prepare an asset account with a sale

## Introduction
### Nature and scope of business economics
= Micro and macro economics, and economics for managerial decisions.
- Business economics applies economic theory to business decisions: demand, cost, pricing, investment.
- Microeconomics studies individual units; macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole.
- Basic problems: what, how and for whom to produce, arising from scarcity.
- Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative forgone.
> Explain the scope of business economics
> Apply opportunity cost to decisions
## Demand Analysis
### Demand and elasticity
= Law of demand, determinants, and price, income and cross elasticity.
- Law of demand: other things equal, quantity demanded rises as price falls.
- Price elasticity Ep = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price; elastic if |Ep| > 1.
- If demand is elastic, a price cut raises total revenue; if inelastic, it lowers it.
- Income elasticity is negative for inferior goods; cross elasticity is positive for substitutes and negative for complements.
> Compute price elasticity of demand
> Relate elasticity to pricing decisions
hook: Petrol prices rise 10% but people buy almost as much. Mangoes rise 10% and sales collapse. Why?
ex: Price rises from ₹10 to ₹12 (20%); quantity falls from 100 to 70 (30%). Ep = 30 ÷ 20 = 1.5: elastic.
q: If Ep = 0.4 and price rises, what happens to total revenue? || It rises, because demand is inelastic.
terms: elasticity; substitutes; complements; inferior goods
## Production and Cost
### Production function and costs
= Laws of variable proportions, returns to scale and cost curves.
- Law of variable proportions: as more of a variable input is added to fixed inputs, marginal product eventually falls.
- Returns to scale (all inputs varied): increasing, constant or decreasing.
- Total cost = Fixed cost + Variable cost; marginal cost = change in total cost from one more unit.
- Short-run average and marginal cost curves are U-shaped; MC cuts AC at its minimum.
> Explain the law of variable proportions
> Derive cost curves from cost data
## Market Structures
### Pricing under different markets
= Perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition and oligopoly.
- Profit is maximised where MR = MC (and MC cuts MR from below).
- Perfect competition: many firms, homogeneous product, price takers (P = MR = AR).
- Monopoly: single seller, price maker; may practise price discrimination.
- Monopolistic competition: differentiated products and selling costs; oligopoly: few interdependent firms (kinked demand curve).
> Apply the MR = MC rule
> Compare market structures
## Macroeconomic Environment
### National income and inflation
= Concepts and measurement of national income, and inflation.
- GDP is the market value of final goods and services produced within a country in a year.
- Methods: product (value added), income and expenditure (C + I + G + (X − M)).
- Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level; India's RBI targets 4% CPI inflation (±2%).
- Business cycle phases: expansion, peak, recession, trough.
> Distinguish GDP, GNP and NNP
> Explain the causes and effects of inflation

## Accounting Standards and IFRS
### Accounting standards in India
= AS, Ind AS and IFRS convergence.
- Ind AS (notified under the Companies Act) are converged with IFRS; they apply in phases to listed and large companies.
- Other companies follow the Accounting Standards (AS) notified for them.
- Key standards: Ind AS 1 (presentation), Ind AS 2 (inventories), Ind AS 16 (property, plant and equipment), Ind AS 115 (revenue).
> Explain why accounting standards matter
> Name key Ind AS and their subjects
## Company Accounts: Shares
### Issue and forfeiture of shares
= Issue at par and premium, calls, forfeiture and reissue.
- Securities premium can be used only for purposes in section 52 (e.g. issuing bonus shares, writing off preliminary expenses).
- Shares are issued at par or premium; issue at a discount is barred except sweat equity.
- On forfeiture, share capital is debited with the called-up amount, and the amount received is credited to share forfeiture.
- On reissue, any discount is debited to forfeiture; the balance of forfeiture on reissued shares goes to capital reserve.
> Pass entries for issue, calls and forfeiture
> Compute the transfer to capital reserve on reissue
ex: 100 shares of ₹10 forfeited after ₹6 paid; reissued at ₹8 fully paid.
ex: Forfeiture ₹600; discount on reissue ₹200; capital reserve ₹400.

= Balance sheet and statement of profit and loss of a company.
- Schedule III (Division I for AS, Division II for Ind AS) prescribes the format.
- Equity and liabilities: shareholders' funds, non-current liabilities, current liabilities.
- Statement of profit and loss shows revenue from operations, other income, expenses, tax and profit for the period.
> Prepare a company balance sheet with notes

= EOQ, stock levels and ABC analysis.

= Time and piece wages, idle time and overtime.

## Introduction
### Nature of international business
= Drivers of globalisation and modes of entry.
- Drivers: market, cost, government and competitive factors.
- Entry modes: exporting, licensing, franchising, contract manufacturing, joint ventures, wholly owned subsidiaries.

## Trade Theories
### Theories of international trade
= Mercantilism, absolute and comparative advantage, and Porter's diamond.
- Absolute advantage (Smith): produce what you make more efficiently than others.
- Comparative advantage (Ricardo): specialise where your opportunity cost is lowest; trade benefits both.
- Porter's diamond: factor conditions, demand conditions, related industries, firm strategy and rivalry.
> Apply comparative advantage
> Use Porter's diamond
## International Institutions
### WTO, IMF and World Bank
= Roles of global economic institutions and trade blocs.
- WTO principles: most-favoured nation, national treatment, transparency.
- The IMF supports balance of payments stability; the World Bank funds development.
- Regional blocs: EU, ASEAN, SAARC, USMCA.
> Explain the role of the WTO
> Distinguish IMF and World Bank roles

### Exchange rates and export procedure
= Exchange rate systems, balance of payments and export documents.
- Balance of payments: current account (trade, services, transfers) and capital/financial account.
- Rupee depreciation makes exports cheaper and imports dearer.
- Export documents: commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, certificate of origin, shipping bill; letters of credit reduce payment risk.
