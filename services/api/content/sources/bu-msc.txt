# msc-fs-1-forensic-science-law | 1 | Forensic Science, Criminal Law and Quality Management
## Criminal Justice System
### Criminal laws of India
= The new criminal codes and the forensic science provisions in them.
- From 1 July 2024 the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) replaced the IPC, the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) the CrPC, and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) the Evidence Act.
- BNSS section 176(3): forensic experts must visit the scene for offences punishable with seven years or more and the collection must be video-recorded.
- BNSS section 349 lets a magistrate order specimen signatures, handwriting, finger impressions and voice samples.
- BSA section 39 makes expert opinion relevant; section 63 governs electronic records.
> Map old and new criminal law provisions relevant to forensics
> Explain the forensic mandates in BNSS
## Expert Testimony
### The expert in court
= Admissibility standards, report writing and cross-examination.
- An expert report states the materials received, methods, results, interpretation and limitations.
- Indian courts treat expert opinion as advisory; the court weighs it with other evidence.
- Frye (general acceptance) and Daubert (testability, peer review, error rate, standards, acceptance) are influential foreign standards.
- Cognitive bias (contextual, confirmation) is reduced by blind verification and sequential unmasking.
> Write a structured expert report
> Identify and reduce cognitive bias
## Quality Management
### Accreditation and quality assurance
= ISO/IEC 17025, validation and proficiency testing.
- ISO/IEC 17025 sets requirements for testing laboratory competence; NABL accredits Indian laboratories.
- Method validation establishes specificity, accuracy, precision, LOD, LOQ, linearity and robustness.
- Proficiency testing and inter-laboratory comparisons check performance.
- Measurement uncertainty must be estimated and reported where relevant.
> Explain the elements of ISO/IEC 17025
> Plan a method validation
## Forensic Statistics
### Interpreting evidence statistically
= Probability, likelihood ratios and fallacies.
- Likelihood ratio LR = P(E | Hp) ÷ P(E | Hd); LR > 1 supports the prosecution proposition.
- Posterior odds = LR × prior odds (Bayes' theorem in odds form).
- Prosecutor's fallacy confuses P(E | Hd) with P(Hd | E).
- Random match probability is the chance that an unrelated person shares the profile.
> Compute and explain a likelihood ratio
> Recognise the prosecutor's fallacy
hook: A blood type found at the scene occurs in 1% of people, and the suspect has it. Is there a 99% chance he is guilty?
ex: P(E | Hp) = 1, P(E | Hd) = 0.01, so LR = 100: the evidence is 100 times more likely if the suspect is the source.
ex: With prior odds 1:1,000, posterior odds = 100 × 1/1,000 = 1:10, far from 99%.
q: What is the prosecutor's fallacy? || Treating the probability of the evidence given innocence as the probability of innocence given the evidence.
terms: likelihood ratio; prior odds; posterior odds; prosecutor's fallacy
@lab probability

# msc-fs-1-instrumental-analysis | 1 | Instrumental Methods in Forensic Analysis
## Chromatography
### GC, HPLC and TLC
= Theory and forensic applications of chromatography.
- Resolution Rs = 2(t₂ − t₁) ÷ (w₁ + w₂); Rs ≥ 1.5 means baseline separation.
- Plate number N = 16(tR ÷ w)²; more plates mean sharper peaks.
- GC suits volatile compounds (accelerants, alcohol, drugs); HPLC non-volatile and thermally labile ones.
> Compute resolution and plate number
> Choose a chromatographic method for a sample
@lab ink-chromatography
## Spectroscopy
### Molecular spectroscopy
= UV-Vis, FTIR, Raman and fluorescence in forensics.
- FTIR and Raman give molecular fingerprints of drugs, fibres, paints and polymers.
- ATR-FTIR analyses solids with little preparation.
- Fluorescence detects body fluids and some inks under alternate light sources.
> Interpret FTIR spectra of common evidence
> Choose a spectroscopic method
@lab beer-lambert
## Mass Spectrometry
### MS and hyphenated techniques
= Ionisation methods, analysers and GC-MS and LC-MS/MS.
- Ionisation: electron impact (hard, library searchable), chemical ionisation and electrospray (soft).
- Analysers: quadrupole, time-of-flight, ion trap, Orbitrap.
- GC-MS is the confirmatory standard for many drugs; LC-MS/MS for trace toxicology.
> Explain how GC-MS confirms a drug
> Compare ionisation methods
## Elemental and Microscopic Analysis
### AAS, ICP, XRF and SEM-EDX
= Elemental analysis and electron microscopy of trace evidence.
- AAS and ICP-MS measure trace metals (poisons, glass, soil).
- XRF is non-destructive elemental analysis.
- SEM-EDX identifies gunshot residue particles (lead, barium, antimony).
> Choose an elemental technique for evidence
> Identify gunshot residue by SEM-EDX

# msc-fs-1-forensic-biology | 1 | Forensic Biology and Serology
## Biological Trace Evidence
### Hair, fibres, pollen and diatoms
= Microscopy and identification of biological traces.
- Hair comparison uses colour, medulla, cuticle scales and cortex; nuclear DNA from roots, mtDNA from shafts.
- Forensic palynology uses pollen to link people and places.
- Diatoms in bone marrow support ante-mortem drowning.
> Compare hairs microscopically
> Use diatom tests in drowning cases
@lab hair-microscopy
@lab fibre-microscopy
## Serology
### Body fluid identification
= Presumptive, confirmatory and immunological tests.
- Blood: Kastle–Meyer, luminol (presumptive); Takayama, Teichmann, immunochromatographic Hb tests (confirmatory).
- Semen: acid phosphatase, PSA, sperm microscopy; saliva: amylase.
- Species origin by precipitin (ring, Ouchterlony double diffusion) tests.
> Plan a sequence of tests for a stain
> Determine species of origin
@lab blood-typing
@lab enzyme-amylase
## Wildlife and Botanical Forensics
### Wildlife crime
= Species identification and the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972.
- Wildlife crime includes poaching and trade in protected species and their parts.
- Species are identified by morphology, microscopy and DNA barcoding (cytochrome b, COI).
- The Wildlife Crime Control Bureau coordinates enforcement in India.
> Identify species from parts using DNA barcoding
> Explain the legal framework for wildlife crime
## Forensic Entomology
### Insects and time since death
= Insect succession and estimating the post-mortem interval.
- Blowflies arrive within minutes to hours; their life cycle is egg, larva (three instars), pupa, adult.
- Accumulated degree hours (ADH) relate development to temperature.
- Succession of insect species estimates longer intervals.
> Estimate a minimum PMI from larval stage and temperature
> Collect entomological evidence

# msc-fs-2-dna-profiling | 2 | Forensic DNA Profiling
## DNA Basics
### DNA structure and variation
= DNA structure, polymorphism and markers used in forensics.
- DNA is a double helix of nucleotides; A pairs with T, G with C.
- STRs (2–6 bp repeats) are highly polymorphic; SNPs are single-base variants.
- CODIS core set has 20 STR loci; amelogenin indicates sex.
> Explain why STRs suit identification
> Describe common forensic markers
@3d dna
## DNA Workflow
### Extraction, quantification and amplification
= Organic, Chelex and silica extraction; qPCR; multiplex PCR.
- Extraction methods: organic (phenol–chloroform), Chelex-100, silica columns and magnetic beads; differential extraction separates sperm and epithelial cells.
- qPCR quantifies human DNA and detects inhibitors and degradation.
- PCR cycles: denaturation (≈94–95 °C), annealing (≈50–65 °C), extension (≈72 °C); copies double each cycle.
> Choose an extraction method
> Explain PCR and qPCR quantification
@lab pcr-amplification
@lab gel-electrophoresis
## Profile Interpretation
### Interpretation and statistics
= Alleles, stutter, mixtures and match statistics.
- Artefacts: stutter, pull-up, drop-in, drop-out and allele imbalance.
- Random match probability by the product rule with Hardy–Weinberg genotype frequencies (p² homozygote, 2pq heterozygote).
- Mixtures are interpreted with probabilistic genotyping software giving likelihood ratios.
> Interpret a single-source profile
> Compute genotype frequencies and an RMP
@lab hardy-weinberg
@lab dna-fingerprinting
ex: At one locus alleles 12 and 15 have frequencies 0.1 and 0.2: genotype frequency 2pq = 0.04.
ex: Across loci, frequencies multiply (product rule), giving very small RMPs.
## Lineage Markers and Applications
### Y-STR, mtDNA and kinship
= Lineage markers, paternity and disaster victim identification.
- Y-STRs follow the paternal line; mtDNA follows the maternal line; neither is unique to an individual.
- Paternity index compares the probability of the child's alleles with the alleged father versus a random man.
- Disaster victim identification combines DNA, fingerprints and dental records.
> Choose lineage markers for a case
> Explain a paternity index

# msc-fs-2-toxicology-drugs | 2 | Forensic Toxicology and Drug Analysis
## Toxicokinetics
### Absorption to elimination
= Pharmacokinetics applied to poisoning and alcohol.
- ADME: absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion.
- Widmark formula: BAC = A ÷ (W × r) − βt (A alcohol in g, W body mass, r distribution factor, β elimination rate).
- Post-mortem redistribution can raise drug concentrations in central blood.
> Estimate blood alcohol with Widmark's formula
> Choose specimens considering redistribution
## Analytical Toxicology
### Screening and confirmation
= Sample preparation, immunoassays and confirmatory analysis.
- Extraction: liquid–liquid, solid-phase extraction, protein precipitation.
- Immunoassays screen urine for drug classes; positives are confirmed by GC-MS or LC-MS/MS.
- Cut-off concentrations decide positive or negative results.
> Design a screening and confirmation scheme
> Explain cut-offs and cross-reactivity
@lab toxicology-spot-tests
## Poisons
### Common poisons in India
= Pesticides, plant poisons, metals and gases.
- Organophosphates inhibit acetylcholinesterase (treatment: atropine and oximes).
- Aluminium phosphide releases phosphine gas.
- Plant poisons: oleander (cardiac glycosides), datura (anticholinergic), abrus (abrin).
> Identify common poisons by symptoms and tests
> Describe the mechanism of organophosphate poisoning
## Drugs of Abuse
### Seized drug analysis
= Sampling, identification and new psychoactive substances.
- Representative sampling (e.g. hypergeometric or Bayesian plans) for large seizures.
- Identification needs at least two independent techniques (SWGDRUG categories).
- New psychoactive substances (synthetic cannabinoids, cathinones) need updated reference standards.
> Plan sampling of a large seizure
> Apply SWGDRUG recommendations

## Handwriting Examination
### Handwriting identification
= Characteristics, standards and conclusions.
- Individual characteristics: letter formation, connections, proportions, alignment, pen pressure, speed.
- Collected (course of business) and requested standards should be contemporaneous and adequate.
- Conclusions range from identification through probable to elimination.

## Document Analysis
### Printed documents, inks and security features
= Printers, counterfeit currency and ink dating.
- Printing processes: offset, inkjet, laser, intaglio (currency).
- Indian banknote features: watermark, security thread, latent image, micro-lettering, intaglio, colour-shifting ink.
- Inks are compared by TLC and spectroscopy; ink dating is uncertain and contested.
> Identify printing processes
> Examine currency security features

## Fingerprint Science
### Ridge detail and development
= Levels of detail, development chemistry and AFIS.
- Level 1: pattern; level 2: minutiae; level 3: pores and ridge edges.
- Chemistry: ninhydrin and DFO react with amino acids; physical developer with lipids; cyanoacrylate polymerises on residues.
- AFIS searches databases (NAFIS in India) and returns candidates for human comparison.
> Choose development techniques in sequence
> Explain AFIS searching

@lab fingerprint-patterns

### Footwear, tyre and tool marks
= Comparison of impressions and marks.
- Class and individual (acquired) characteristics.
- Gel lifters and electrostatic lifters recover dust impressions.

## Firearms
### Firearms and ammunition
= Classification, mechanisms and cartridge components.
- Cartridge: case, primer, propellant, projectile.
- Rifling: number, direction and twist of lands and grooves are class characteristics.
- Marks on cartridge cases: firing pin, breech face, ejector and extractor marks.

## Ballistics
### Internal, external and terminal ballistics
= Projectile motion, wound ballistics and range of fire.
- Kinetic energy KE = ½mv² largely determines wounding potential.
- External ballistics: trajectory, drag and ricochet.
- Range of fire from soot, stippling and pellet spread (shotguns).
> Compute muzzle energy
> Estimate range of fire

@lab projectile-motion
## Gunshot Residue
### GSR analysis
= Formation, collection and analysis of GSR.
- GSR contains lead, barium and antimony particles from the primer.
- Collection by stub lifts from hands within hours of firing.
- SEM-EDX identifies characteristic particles by morphology and composition.
> Collect and analyse GSR
> Interpret GSR findings cautiously
## Trace Physical Evidence
### Glass, paint and soil
= Physical and chemical comparison of trace evidence.
- Glass: refractive index (GRIM), density, elemental composition (LA-ICP-MS).
- Paint layer sequences are compared by microscopy and FTIR.
- Soil: colour (Munsell), particle size, minerals.

# msc-fs-3-digital-forensics | 3 | Digital Forensics | year=2025-26
## Digital Evidence Principles
### Standards for digital evidence
= ACPO principles, ISO/IEC 27037 and legal admissibility.
- ACPO principles: no action should change data; competent persons; audit trail; overall responsibility.
- ISO/IEC 27037 guides identification, collection, acquisition and preservation.
- BSA section 63 requires a certificate for electronic records.
> Apply the ACPO principles
> Prepare admissible digital evidence
## Computer Forensics
### Acquisition and file system analysis
= Imaging, hashing and analysis of NTFS and FAT.
- Write blockers prevent changes; images in E01 or raw (dd) formats.
- NTFS stores file records in the MFT; deleted entries may persist.
- Timeline analysis uses MAC(B) timestamps.
> Image and verify a drive
> Recover deleted NTFS files
## Mobile and Cloud Forensics
### Mobile devices and cloud data
= Extraction methods, encryption and cloud evidence.
- Full-disk and file-based encryption limit physical extraction.
- App data (SQLite databases) holds messages, locations and media.
- Cloud evidence is obtained through legal process and account access with authorisation.
> Choose extraction methods for a phone
> Explain challenges of cloud evidence
## Network and Memory Forensics
### Volatile and network evidence
= RAM analysis and network investigation.
- Memory images reveal running processes, network connections, keys and malware (Volatility framework).
- Logs (firewall, proxy, server) and NetFlow reconstruct activity.
- Attribution must account for VPNs, Tor and spoofing.
> Analyse a memory image
> Reconstruct events from logs

# msc-fs-3-forensic-medicine-anthropology | 3 | Forensic Medicine, Anthropology and Odontology | year=2025-26
## Thanatology
### Death and post-mortem interval
= Modes of death and estimation of time since death.
- Algor mortis (Henssge nomogram), livor mortis, rigor mortis, vitreous potassium.
- Decomposition is accelerated by heat and slowed by cold or burial.
- Autopsy determines cause, manner and mechanism of death.
> Estimate PMI by multiple methods
> Distinguish cause, manner and mechanism of death
## Injuries and Trauma
### Wound analysis
= Blunt, sharp and firearm injuries and ante-mortem vs post-mortem wounds.
- Vital reaction (inflammation, bleeding) indicates ante-mortem injury.
- Wound age is estimated from healing stages.
- Pattern injuries can reflect the weapon.
> Distinguish ante-mortem and post-mortem injuries
> Relate injuries to weapons
## Forensic Anthropology
### The biological profile
= Estimating sex, age, stature and ancestry from skeletal remains.
- Sex: pelvis is most reliable (sub-pubic angle, greater sciatic notch), then skull.
- Age: epiphyseal fusion, pubic symphysis, auricular surface, cranial sutures.
- Stature from long bones with population-specific regression equations.
> Build a biological profile from bones
> Distinguish human and animal bone
@3d skeleton
## Forensic Odontology
### Dental identification and bite marks
= Dental charting, age estimation and bite mark analysis.
- Comparison of ante-mortem and post-mortem dental records (Interpol DVI forms).
- Dental age from eruption and development stages (e.g. Demirjian method).
- Bite mark analysis has high error rates and is now treated with great caution.
> Identify persons from dental records
> Explain limits of bite mark evidence
