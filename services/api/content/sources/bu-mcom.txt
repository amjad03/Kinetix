# mcom-1-organisational-behaviour | 1 | Management Concepts and Organisational Behaviour
## Management Thought
### Approaches to management
= Classical, behavioural, quantitative, systems and contingency approaches.
- Classical: scientific management (Taylor), administrative (Fayol), bureaucratic (Weber).
- Behavioural: human relations (Mayo), Maslow, McGregor.
- Systems and contingency approaches treat management as situational and open to the environment.
> Compare management approaches
> Apply a contingency view to a case
## Individual Behaviour
### Perception, personality and learning
= Individual determinants of behaviour at work.
- Perceptual shortcuts: halo effect, stereotyping, contrast effect, projection.
- Personality: Big Five; locus of control (internal vs external).
- Reinforcement schedules: continuous, fixed/variable interval, fixed/variable ratio.
> Identify perceptual errors
> Choose a reinforcement schedule
## Motivation and Leadership
### Motivation and leadership theories
= Content and process theories and leadership models.
- Herzberg, McClelland, Vroom (M = E × I × V), Adams (equity), Locke (goal setting).
- Leadership: trait, behavioural, situational (Hersey–Blanchard), path–goal, transformational.
- Emotional intelligence (Goleman): self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skill.
> Apply motivation theories
> Choose a leadership approach
## Group Dynamics and Change
### Groups, conflict and change
= Teams, conflict and organisational change and development.
- Group cohesiveness raises productivity when group norms support organisational goals.
- Conflict can be functional or dysfunctional.
- Organisational development interventions: sensitivity training, survey feedback, team building.
> Manage conflict constructively
> Plan an OD intervention

# mcom-1-managerial-economics | 1 | Managerial Economics
## Demand and Supply
### Demand analysis and forecasting
= Elasticities, demand estimation and forecasting.
- Point elasticity e = (dQ/dP)(P/Q).
- Demand estimation by regression: Q = a + bP + cY + …
- Forecasting: survey methods, trend projection, barometric indicators, econometric models.
> Estimate and interpret elasticities
> Forecast demand with a trend equation
## Production and Costs
### Production and cost analysis
= Isoquants, economies of scope and break-even analysis.
- An isoquant shows input combinations giving the same output; MRTS = MPL ÷ MPK.
- Economies of scope: cheaper to produce products together than separately.
- Learning curve: unit cost falls by a constant percentage each time cumulative output doubles.
> Find the optimal input combination
> Apply learning curve effects
## Pricing
### Pricing strategies and market structures
= Pricing under competition and monopoly, and pricing practices.
- Monopoly mark-up: (P − MC) ÷ P = −1 ÷ e (Lerner index).
- Third-degree price discrimination charges more in the market with less elastic demand.
- Game theory: Nash equilibrium is a set of strategies where no player gains by changing alone.
> Price with the Lerner rule
> Find a Nash equilibrium
## Macro Environment
### Macroeconomic policy and business
= Monetary and fiscal policy, inflation and business cycles.
- Inflation types: demand-pull, cost-push; Phillips curve trade-off in the short run.
- RBI's monetary policy committee sets the repo rate to target 4% CPI inflation within a 2–6% band.
- Fiscal deficit = Total expenditure − (Revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts).
> Explain how policy affects business
> Compute the fiscal deficit

# mcom-1-advanced-cost-accounting | 1 | Advanced Cost and Management Accounting
## Activity-Based Costing
### ABC system
= Activities, cost drivers and ABC vs traditional costing.
- ABC assigns overheads to activities, then to products by cost drivers (set-ups, orders, inspections).
- Activity cost driver rate = Activity cost pool ÷ Cost driver volume.
- ABC corrects cross-subsidisation where low-volume products are under-costed.
> Compute product costs under ABC
> Compare ABC with traditional absorption costing
hook: A factory makes 10,000 standard shirts and 100 custom ones. Why might the custom shirts look profitable but not be?
ex: Set-up cost ₹60,000 for 30 set-ups: ₹2,000 per set-up. Custom line uses 20 set-ups = ₹40,000 for 100 shirts: ₹400 each.
ex: Spread by labour hours instead, the custom shirts might get only ₹20 each of set-up cost.
q: What is a cost driver? || A factor whose change causes a change in the cost of an activity.
terms: activity; cost pool; cost driver; cross-subsidisation
## Strategic Cost Management
### Target, life cycle and kaizen costing
= Modern costing techniques for strategy.
- Target cost = Target price − Target profit.
- Life cycle costing covers costs from design to disposal.
- Kaizen costing seeks continuous small cost reductions during production.
> Compute target cost and cost gap
> Explain life cycle costing
## Decision Making
### Relevant costing
= Relevant costs, make or buy, special orders and limiting factors.
- Relevant costs are future, incremental cash costs; sunk costs are irrelevant.
- Opportunity cost is relevant when resources are scarce.
- With one limiting factor, rank products by contribution per unit of that factor.
> Make make-or-buy and special order decisions
> Allocate scarce resources

## Performance Measurement
### Responsibility accounting and transfer pricing
= Responsibility centres, ROI, residual income and transfer pricing.
- Centres: cost, revenue, profit and investment centres.
- ROI = Operating profit ÷ Capital employed; residual income = Profit − (Capital × Cost of capital).
- Transfer price range: minimum = marginal cost + opportunity cost; maximum = external market price.
> Evaluate divisions by ROI and RI
> Set a transfer price

# mcom-2-corporate-financial-reporting | 2 | Corporate Financial Reporting
## Ind AS Framework
### Ind AS and IFRS
= Conceptual framework and key Ind AS.
- Ind AS 1 presentation; Ind AS 8 accounting policies, estimates and errors; Ind AS 10 events after the reporting period.
- Ind AS 115 revenue: five steps — identify contract, performance obligations, transaction price, allocate, recognise.
- Ind AS 116 leases: lessees recognise right-of-use assets and lease liabilities for most leases.
> Apply the five-step revenue model
> Account for a lease under Ind AS 116
## Business Combinations
### Ind AS 103 and consolidation
= Acquisition method, goodwill and consolidated statements.
- Acquisition method: identify acquirer, acquisition date, measure assets and liabilities at fair value, recognise goodwill or bargain purchase.
- Goodwill = Consideration + NCI + previously held interest − Net identifiable assets.
- Ind AS 110: control means power, exposure to variable returns and ability to use power to affect returns.
> Compute goodwill on acquisition
> Prepare a simple consolidated balance sheet
## Financial Instruments
### Ind AS 109 and 32
= Classification, measurement and impairment of financial instruments.
- Financial assets: amortised cost, FVOCI or FVTPL depending on business model and cash flow characteristics.
- Expected credit loss model for impairment.
- Compound instruments (convertible debentures) are split into liability and equity components.
> Classify financial assets
> Split a compound instrument
## Corporate Reporting
### Integrated and sustainability reporting
= Non-financial reporting frameworks.
- BRSR is mandatory for the top 1,000 listed companies; BRSR Core needs reasonable assurance for the top companies in phases.
- Integrated reporting (IIRC) covers six capitals.
- ISSB standards (IFRS S1, S2) set global sustainability disclosure baselines.
> Explain BRSR requirements
> Describe the six capitals

## Research Design
### Research problem and design
= Problem formulation, hypotheses and designs.
- Research questions should be specific and answerable.
- Designs: exploratory, descriptive, causal.
- Variables: independent, dependent, moderating, mediating.
> Formulate hypotheses

## Sampling and Measurement
### Sampling and scales
= Sampling techniques, sample size and measurement validity.
- n = z²σ² ÷ e² for estimating a mean.
- Validity types: content, construct, criterion; reliability: test–retest, internal consistency.
- Scales: Likert, semantic differential, rank order.
> Determine sample size
> Assess reliability and validity
## Data Analysis
### Parametric and non-parametric tests
= t, F, chi-square and non-parametric tests.
- Paired t-test compares before–after means.
- Chi-square test of independence: χ² = Σ (O − E)² ÷ E with (r − 1)(c − 1) df.
- Non-parametric: sign test, Wilcoxon, Mann–Whitney, Kruskal–Wallis.
> Choose and run a suitable test
> Interpret test output
ex: Observed 30 and 20 against expected 25 and 25: χ² = 25/25 + 25/25 = 2; with 1 df, below 3.84, so not significant at 5%.

### Research report

- Structure: introduction, review, methodology, analysis, findings, conclusions.

= Globalisation, trade theories and protectionism.
- Comparative advantage and Heckscher–Ohlin factor endowment theory.

= Types and theories of FDI and India's FDI policy.
- FDI types: horizontal, vertical, conglomerate; greenfield vs brownfield.

### Exchange rates and risk
= Exchange rate determination and exposure management.
- Purchasing power parity: exchange rate changes offset inflation differentials.
- Interest rate parity: F = S × (1 + i_d) ÷ (1 + i_f).
- Hedging: forwards, futures, options and natural hedges.
> Compute forward rates by interest rate parity
> Hedge transaction exposure

- Foreign Trade Policy 2023 aims at export growth and ease of doing business; DGFT administers it.
- Export promotion: SEZs, EOUs, export credit (ECGC, EXIM Bank).
- WTO agreements: GATT, GATS, TRIPS, TRIMs.

## Tax Planning Concepts
### Tax planning, avoidance and evasion
= Distinguishing planning, avoidance and evasion, and GAAR.
- Tax planning uses provisions as intended; avoidance exploits loopholes; evasion is illegal.
- GAAR (Chapter X-A) lets authorities deny benefits of impermissible avoidance arrangements.
- McDowell and Vodafone judgments shaped the Indian position on avoidance.
> Distinguish planning, avoidance and evasion
> Explain GAAR
## Corporate Taxation
### Taxation of companies
= Corporate tax rates, MAT and concessional regimes.
- Section 115BAA: 22% (effective about 25.17%) for domestic companies forgoing certain deductions; MAT does not apply.
- Section 115BAB (new manufacturing companies) 15% for companies set up before its sunset date.
- MAT (section 115JB) at 15% of book profit for companies under the normal regime.
> Compute a company's tax liability
> Compare tax regimes for companies
## Tax Planning for Decisions
### Tax-based managerial decisions
= Make or buy, lease or buy, and capital structure.
- Interest is tax-deductible; dividends are not, so debt gives a tax shield (Interest × tax rate).
- Lease or buy: compare after-tax present values of costs.
- Depreciation tax shield = Depreciation × tax rate.
> Compare lease and buy after tax
> Value a tax shield
## Assessment and Procedures
### Returns, assessment and appeals
= Filing returns, assessment types and appeals.
- Assessment types: summary (143(1)), scrutiny (143(3)), best judgement (144), reassessment (147–148A).
- Faceless assessment and appeals schemes.
- Appeals: CIT(A), ITAT, High Court, Supreme Court.
> Describe assessment procedures
> Trace the appeal route

# mcom-3-advanced-financial-management | 3 | Advanced Financial Management (Finance elective) | year=2025-26
## Valuation
### Corporate valuation
= DCF, relative valuation and EVA.
- Free cash flow to firm = EBIT(1 − t) + Depreciation − Capex − ΔWorking capital.
- Firm value = PV of FCFF at WACC + terminal value (FCFF × (1 + g) ÷ (WACC − g)).
- EVA = NOPAT − WACC × Capital employed.
> Value a firm by DCF
> Compute EVA
## Mergers and Acquisitions
### M&A analysis
= Synergy, exchange ratios and takeover regulation.
- Synergy = V(AB) − (V(A) + V(B)).
- Exchange ratio based on EPS, market price or book value.
- SEBI SAST Regulations: open offer triggered at 25% voting rights.
> Compute synergy and exchange ratios
> Explain takeover rules
## Risk Management
### Derivatives for corporate risk
= Using futures, options and swaps to manage risk.
- Hedge ratio for minimum variance: h = ρ σS ÷ σF.
- Options give the right, not the obligation; premiums are the cost of protection.
- Interest rate swaps change fixed to floating exposure or vice versa.
> Compute a minimum-variance hedge ratio
> Choose a hedging instrument
## Financial Restructuring
### Restructuring and insolvency
= Buybacks, demergers and the IBC.
- Buyback limit: 25% of paid-up capital and free reserves (with debt–equity not above 2:1 after buyback).
- Demergers separate business units into new companies.
- IBC: corporate insolvency resolution within 180 days (extendable to 330 days including litigation).
> Evaluate a buyback
> Describe the insolvency resolution process

# mcom-4-indirect-taxes | 4 | Indirect Taxes: GST and Customs | year=2025-26

## GST Framework
### Constitutional and legal framework
= GST laws, the GST Council and dual structure.
- Laws: CGST Act, SGST Acts, IGST Act, UTGST Act, GST (Compensation to States) Act.
- Article 246A gives both Centre and States power to levy GST.
- Inter-state supplies attract IGST, settled between Centre and States.
> Explain the legal framework of GST
> Classify supplies for CGST, SGST or IGST
## Valuation and ITC
### Value of supply and input tax credit
= Transaction value, inclusions and ITC rules.
- Value of supply is the transaction value plus taxes other than GST, incidental expenses and subsidies linked to price (section 15).
- Discounts before or at supply recorded on the invoice are excluded.
- ITC is reversed if payment to the supplier is not made within 180 days.
> Compute the value of supply
> Compute eligible ITC
## Procedures
### Registration, invoicing, returns and e-invoicing
= Compliance under GST.
- E-invoicing applies above a turnover threshold (₹5 crore).
- Reverse charge applies to notified supplies (e.g. GTA, legal services to businesses).
- Annual return GSTR-9; reconciliation statement GSTR-9C for larger taxpayers.
> Prepare a compliance calendar
> Identify reverse charge supplies
## Customs
### Customs law
= Types of customs duty and valuation of imports.
- Basic customs duty, IGST on imports, social welfare surcharge, safeguard and anti-dumping duties.
- Assessable value = CIF value (transaction value + freight + insurance).
- Baggage, warehousing and drawback provisions.
> Compute customs duty on an import
> Explain duty drawback
