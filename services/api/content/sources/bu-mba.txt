# mba-1-management-ob | 1 | Management and Organisational Behaviour
## Management Concepts
### Evolution of management thought
= Classical, neo-classical, systems and contingency theories.
- Classical: Taylor (scientific management), Fayol (administrative principles), Weber (bureaucracy).
- Neo-classical: Hawthorne studies, human relations, McGregor's Theory X and Y.
- Systems theory views the organisation as an open system of interrelated parts; contingency theory says the best approach depends on the situation.
- Drucker emphasised management by objectives and knowledge workers.
> Trace the evolution of management thought
> Apply contingency thinking to a case
## Individual Behaviour
### Personality, perception and attitudes
= Personality theories, attribution and job attitudes.
- Big Five and MBTI are common personality frameworks.
- Attribution theory: behaviour is judged as internally or externally caused using distinctiveness, consensus and consistency; the fundamental attribution error overweights internal causes.
- Job satisfaction, organisational commitment and engagement predict performance and turnover.
> Use attribution theory to explain judgements of others
> Link attitudes to work outcomes
### Motivation theories
= Content and process theories of motivation.
- Content: Maslow, Herzberg's two-factor theory, McClelland's needs for achievement, affiliation and power, Alderfer's ERG.
- Process: Vroom's expectancy (M = E × I × V), Adams' equity theory, Locke's goal-setting theory.
- Goal-setting: specific, difficult goals with feedback lead to higher performance.
> Compare content and process theories
> Design a motivation scheme for a team
## Group and Leadership
### Leadership theories
= Trait, behavioural, contingency and transformational leadership.
- Behavioural studies: Ohio State (initiating structure, consideration), Michigan (employee- vs production-oriented), Blake and Mouton's managerial grid.
- Fiedler's contingency model matches style (LPC) to situational favourableness; Hersey–Blanchard matches style to follower readiness.
- Transformational leaders inspire and change followers; transactional leaders exchange rewards for performance.
> Compare leadership theories
> Choose a leadership style for a situation
## Organisational Dynamics
### Culture, power and change
= Organisational culture, power and politics, and change management.
- Schein's levels of culture: artefacts, espoused values, basic assumptions.
- French and Raven's bases of power: legitimate, reward, coercive, expert, referent.
- Kotter's eight steps to lead change begin with creating urgency and end with anchoring changes in culture.
> Diagnose an organisation's culture
> Plan change with Kotter's model

# mba-1-managerial-economics | 1 | Managerial Economics
## Demand and Forecasting
### Demand analysis and elasticity
= Demand functions, elasticities and their managerial use.
- Point price elasticity e = (dQ/dP) × (P/Q); arc elasticity uses average P and Q.
- Total revenue is maximised where price elasticity equals −1 (MR = 0).
- MR = P(1 + 1/e), linking pricing to elasticity.
- Advertising and income elasticity guide promotion and market expansion.
> Compute point and arc elasticity
> Relate elasticity to marginal revenue and pricing
hook: A cinema raises ticket prices and earns less. What did it not know about its customers?
ex: Q = 100 − 2P. At P = 20, Q = 60; e = −2 × 20 ÷ 60 = −0.67 (inelastic): raising price raises revenue.
ex: Revenue is maximum where e = −1: P = 25, Q = 50, TR = ₹1,250.
q: If e = −2 and P = ₹100, what is MR? || MR = 100(1 − 1/2) = ₹50.
terms: point elasticity; marginal revenue; revenue maximisation
### Demand forecasting
= Survey and statistical methods of forecasting demand.
- Survey methods: buyers' intentions, expert opinion (Delphi), sales force composite.
- Statistical methods: trend projection, moving averages, exponential smoothing, regression.
- Exponential smoothing: Fₜ₊₁ = αAₜ + (1 − α)Fₜ.
> Choose a forecasting method
> Forecast with exponential smoothing
## Production and Cost
### Production and cost functions
= Cobb–Douglas production, economies of scale and cost curves.
- Cobb–Douglas: Q = A Lᵅ Kᵝ; α + β > 1, = 1 or < 1 gives increasing, constant or decreasing returns to scale.
- Least-cost combination: MPL ÷ w = MPK ÷ r.
- Long-run average cost is the envelope of short-run curves; economies of scale lower it.
> Identify returns to scale from a production function
> Find the least-cost input combination
## Market Structure and Pricing
### Pricing under market structures
= Equilibrium in competitive, monopoly and oligopoly markets, and pricing practices.
- Profit maximisation: MR = MC.
- A monopolist with constant MC charges P = MC ÷ (1 + 1/e).
- Oligopoly models: Cournot (quantity), Bertrand (price), kinked demand; game theory and the prisoners' dilemma.
- Pricing practices: cost-plus, peak-load, transfer pricing, price discrimination.
> Find the profit-maximising price and output
> Analyse a duopoly as a game
## Macroeconomics for Managers
### National income, inflation and policy
= GDP, business cycles, and monetary and fiscal policy.
- GDP = C + I + G + (X − M).
- Monetary policy (RBI): repo rate, CRR, SLR, open market operations; fiscal policy: taxes and government spending.
- The multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC).
> Explain the effect of policy on business
> Compute the investment multiplier

## Probability
### Probability and Bayes' theorem
= Rules of probability, conditional probability and Bayes' theorem.
- P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B); P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) ÷ P(B).
- Bayes: P(A | B) = P(B | A) P(A) ÷ P(B).
- Independent events: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) P(B).
> Apply conditional probability to business cases
> Revise probabilities with Bayes' theorem
hook: A test is 95% accurate and 1% of products are defective. If the test flags a product, how likely is it really defective?
ex: P(D) = 0.01, P(+|D) = 0.95, P(+|not D) = 0.05. P(+) = 0.0095 + 0.0495 = 0.059.
ex: P(D|+) = 0.0095 ÷ 0.059 ≈ 0.16: only 16%.
q: P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4, independent. P(A and B)? || 0.2
terms: conditional probability; prior; posterior; Bayes' theorem

= Central limit theorem, standard error and interval estimates.

- 95% confidence interval for μ: x̄ ± 1.96 σ ÷ √n (use t with n − 1 df when σ is unknown and n small).
- Sample size for a mean: n = (zσ ÷ E)².

## Law of Contract
### Indian Contract Act, 1872
= Essentials of a valid contract, void and voidable agreements, and remedies.
- A contract is an agreement enforceable by law (section 2(h)); essentials: offer and acceptance, consideration, capacity, free consent, lawful object, not expressly void.
- A minor's agreement is void (Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose).
- Consent caused by coercion, undue influence, fraud or misrepresentation makes a contract voidable.
- Remedies for breach: damages (Hadley v Baxendale), specific performance, injunction, quantum meruit.
> Test the validity of a contract
> Identify remedies for breach
## Sale of Goods and Negotiable Instruments
### Sale of goods and negotiable instruments
= Conditions and warranties, caveat emptor, and cheques and bills.
- Sale of Goods Act, 1930: a condition is essential to the contract; a warranty is collateral.
- Caveat emptor (let the buyer beware) has exceptions such as fitness for purpose and merchantable quality.
- Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881: promissory note, bill of exchange, cheque; section 138 makes dishonour of a cheque for insufficient funds an offence.
> Distinguish conditions and warranties
> Explain the consequences of cheque dishonour
## Company Law
### Companies Act, 2013 essentials
= Incorporation, directors, meetings and corporate governance.
- Corporate social responsibility (section 135): companies above set thresholds (net worth ₹500 crore, turnover ₹1,000 crore or net profit ₹5 crore) spend 2% of average net profit.
- Independent directors and audit committees strengthen governance.
- NCLT handles company law matters; IBC 2016 governs insolvency resolution.
> Explain key governance provisions
> State the CSR obligation
## Consumer and Competition Law
### Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and Competition Act, 2002
= Consumer rights, redressal commissions and anti-competitive practices.
- Consumer commissions' pecuniary jurisdiction: district up to ₹50 lakh, state ₹50 lakh–₹2 crore, national above ₹2 crore (by value of goods or services paid).
- The Central Consumer Protection Authority acts against unfair trade practices and misleading advertisements.
- The Competition Act prohibits anti-competitive agreements, abuse of dominance and regulates combinations; the CCI enforces it.
> Identify the right consumer forum
> Recognise anti-competitive practices
## Information Technology and IPR
### IT Act and intellectual property
= Electronic contracts, data protection and intellectual property rights.
- The IT Act, 2000 recognises electronic records and digital signatures.
- The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 governs processing of personal data with consent.

# mba-2-financial-management | 2 | Financial Management
## Valuation and Risk
### Risk, return and CAPM
= Measuring risk and return, diversification and the capital asset pricing model.
- Expected return E(R) = Σ pᵢ Rᵢ; risk is the standard deviation of returns.
- Diversification removes unsystematic risk; systematic (market) risk remains, measured by beta.
- CAPM: E(Rᵢ) = Rf + βᵢ(Rm − Rf).
- A stock is undervalued if its expected return exceeds the CAPM required return.
> Compute expected return and risk
> Apply CAPM to find required return
hook: Why does a power utility's share move less than the Sensex, while a tech share swings more?
ex: Rf = 7%, Rm = 13%, β = 1.5: required return = 7 + 1.5 × 6 = 16%.
q: What is the required return on a stock with β = 0? || The risk-free rate.
terms: beta; systematic risk; CAPM; market risk premium
### Valuation of bonds and shares
= Present value models for bonds and equity.
- Bond value = Σ C ÷ (1 + k)ᵗ + F ÷ (1 + k)ⁿ; bond prices fall when yields rise.
- Gordon growth model: P₀ = D₁ ÷ (k − g), for k > g.
- Yield to maturity is the discount rate equating bond price and its cash flows.
> Value bonds and shares
> Explain the price–yield relationship
## Capital Budgeting
### Capital budgeting under certainty and risk
= NPV, IRR, MIRR and risk analysis.
- NPV and IRR can conflict for mutually exclusive projects; NPV is preferred.
- MIRR assumes reinvestment at the cost of capital.
- Risk techniques: sensitivity analysis, scenario analysis, decision trees, risk-adjusted discount rate, certainty equivalents.
> Evaluate projects with NPV, IRR and MIRR
> Analyse project risk
## Cost of Capital and Capital Structure
### WACC and capital structure theories
= Cost of each source, WACC, and NI, NOI, traditional and MM approaches.
- WACC = Σ wᵢkᵢ, using market value weights where possible.
- MM without taxes: firm value is independent of capital structure; with taxes, V_L = V_U + tD.
- Trade-off theory balances tax shields against financial distress costs; pecking order prefers internal funds, then debt, then equity.
> Compute WACC
> Compare capital structure theories
## Dividend and Working Capital
### Dividend policy and working capital management
= Dividend models, and managing cash, receivables and inventory.
- Walter: if r > k retain all earnings; if r < k pay out all.
- MM dividend irrelevance holds in perfect markets.
- Receivables policy weighs extra contribution from credit sales against the cost of funds and bad debts.
- Cash conversion cycle = Inventory days + Receivable days − Payable days.
> Apply dividend models
> Evaluate a credit policy change
