## Theoretical Framework of Accounting
### Accounting concepts, conventions and standards
= The basic assumptions and principles of accounting and the role of accounting standards.
- Accounting identifies, measures, records, classifies, summarises and communicates financial information.
- Concepts: business entity, money measurement, going concern, accrual, dual aspect (Assets = Liabilities + Capital), consistency, matching, cost.
- Conventions: conservatism (prudence), materiality, full disclosure, consistency.
- In India, Ind AS (converged with IFRS) apply to listed and large companies; other entities follow the AS issued by the ICAI.
> Explain the main accounting concepts and conventions
> State why accounting standards are needed
hook: A shop owner lends ₹10,000 of his own money to his shop. Is that the shop's money or his?
q: Which concept says a business is separate from its owner? || The business entity concept.
q: Which convention says "anticipate no profit, provide for all losses"? || Conservatism (prudence).
terms: going concern; accrual; matching; Ind AS
## Single Entry System
### Conversion of single entry into double entry
= Finding profit by the statement of affairs method and converting incomplete records.
- Single entry is an incomplete system: usually only cash and personal accounts are kept.
- Statement of affairs = Assets − Liabilities = Capital at a date.
- Profit = Closing capital + Drawings − Additional capital − Opening capital.
- Missing figures (credit sales, credit purchases) are found from total debtors, total creditors and cash accounts.
> Find profit by the statement of affairs method
> Prepare final accounts from incomplete records
ex: Opening capital ₹1,00,000, closing capital ₹1,40,000, drawings ₹20,000, additional capital ₹15,000.
ex: Profit = 1,40,000 + 20,000 − 15,000 − 1,00,000 = ₹45,000.
q: Why is drawings added back to closing capital? || Drawings reduced capital but are not a loss, so they are added back to find the profit earned.
## Consignment Accounts
### Consignment and valuation of stock
= Accounting by consignor and consignee, commission and the valuation of unsold stock.
- In a consignment the consignor sends goods to an agent (consignee) who sells them on commission; ownership stays with the consignor.
- Pro forma invoice shows the goods' details; account sales is the consignee's statement of sales, expenses and commission.
- Del credere commission is extra commission for bearing the risk of bad debts.
- Unsold stock is valued at cost plus a proportion of non-recurring expenses (freight, insurance, carriage to the consignee's place); selling expenses are not added.
- Normal loss is absorbed in the cost of the remaining goods; abnormal loss is credited to the consignment account and charged to profit and loss.
> Prepare the consignment account and consignee's account
> Value closing stock with normal and abnormal losses
## Hire Purchase System
### Hire purchase accounting
= Hire purchase and instalment purchase, interest calculation and journal entries.
- Under hire purchase the hirer gets possession at once but ownership passes only on payment of the last instalment.
- Under an instalment sale, ownership passes at once.
- Cash price = Total instalments − Total interest; interest is charged on the outstanding cash price.
- The hirer records the asset at full cash price and charges depreciation on it.
> Calculate interest when the rate is given
> Pass journal entries in the books of the hirer and vendor
ex: Cash price ₹30,000; down payment ₹6,000; balance in 3 annual instalments at 10% interest.
ex: Year 1 interest = 10% × 24,000 = ₹2,400.
## Accounting for Sale on Approval or Return and Royalty
### Sale or return
= Recording goods sent on sale or return basis.
- Goods sent on approval are not a sale until the customer accepts them or the return period ends.
- When few such transactions occur, entries are reversed at year end for goods not yet approved.
- Unapproved goods are included in closing stock at cost (or net realisable value if lower).
> Record goods sent on sale or return
> Adjust closing stock for unapproved goods
### Royalty accounts
= Royalty, minimum rent, short workings and their recoupment.
- Royalty is payment to the owner of a right (mine, patent, copyright) based on output or sales.
- Minimum rent (dead rent) is payable even if the royalty is lower.
- Short working = Minimum rent − Actual royalty; it can be recouped from later excess royalty within the agreed period.
- Short workings not recouped within the period are written off to profit and loss.
> Prepare the royalty analytical table
> Pass entries in the lessee's books

## Introduction to Companies
### Company: meaning, features and kinds
= Definition under the Companies Act, 2013, features and kinds of companies.
- Section 2(20) defines a company as one incorporated under this Act or any previous company law.
- Features: separate legal entity, perpetual succession, limited liability, common seal (optional since 2015), transferable shares.
- A private company needs at least 2 members (maximum 200) and 2 directors; a public company at least 7 members and 3 directors.
- A one person company (section 2(62)) has one member; small companies and producer companies are other kinds.
> State the features of a company
> Distinguish private, public and one person companies
## Formation of a Company
### Incorporation, MOA and AOA
= Promotion, incorporation, the memorandum and articles of association.
- A promoter conceives the idea and takes steps to form the company; pre-incorporation contracts do not bind the company unless adopted.
- The memorandum (MOA) has clauses: name, registered office, objects, liability, capital and subscription.
- The articles (AOA) are the internal rules; they are subordinate to the MOA and the Act.
- Doctrine of indoor management protects outsiders dealing in good faith; constructive notice assumes they know the public documents.
- Acts beyond the objects clause are ultra vires and void (Ashbury Railway Carriage v Riche).
> Describe the stages of company formation
> Distinguish MOA and AOA
### Prospectus
= Meaning, contents and liability for misstatement.
- A prospectus (section 2(70)) invites the public to subscribe for securities.
- A shelf prospectus is valid for one year; a red herring prospectus omits the price or quantity.
- A statement in lieu of prospectus is filed by a company that does not invite the public.
- Misstatement attracts civil liability (section 35) and criminal liability (section 34).
> Explain kinds of prospectus
> State the liability for misstatement
## Company Management
### Directors
= Appointment, qualifications, duties and kinds of directors.
- A public company needs at least 3 directors, a private company 2 and an OPC 1; the maximum is 15 unless a special resolution allows more.
- Every director needs a DIN; at least one director must have stayed in India 182 days in the previous calendar year.
- Listed public companies must have at least one-third independent directors (section 149).
- Certain classes of companies must have at least one woman director.
> State the rules on number and appointment of directors
> Explain the duties of directors
## Company Meetings and Winding Up
### Meetings and resolutions
= Kinds of meetings, quorum, and ordinary and special resolutions.
- AGM (section 96): first within 9 months of the end of the first financial year; then within 6 months of year end, with no more than 15 months between two AGMs.
- Quorum of a public company (section 103): 5 members if up to 1,000 members, 15 if 1,000–5,000, 30 above 5,000; private company: 2.
- The board meets within 30 days of incorporation and at least four times a year, with no more than 120 days between meetings.
- Ordinary resolution: votes for exceed votes against; special resolution: votes for are at least three times votes against.
> State the rules for convening meetings
> Distinguish ordinary and special resolutions
### Winding up
= Modes of winding up and the role of the liquidator.
- Under the Companies Act, 2013 a company is wound up by the Tribunal (NCLT); voluntary winding up is under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (section 59).
- Grounds for winding up by the Tribunal include a special resolution, acts against sovereignty, fraud and failure to file accounts for five years.
- The official liquidator or company liquidator realises the assets and pays creditors in the order of priority.
> Explain the modes of winding up
> List the grounds for winding up by the Tribunal

## Branch Accounts
### Dependent branches
= Debtors system and stock and debtors system for branches.
- A dependent branch keeps no full books; the head office records its transactions.
- Under the debtors system, a branch account (nominal in nature) shows the branch's profit or loss.
- When goods are invoiced at a price above cost, the loading is removed: loading = invoice price × mark-up ÷ (100 + mark-up).
- Under the stock and debtors system separate branch stock, debtors, expenses and adjustment accounts are kept.
> Prepare a branch account under the debtors system
> Remove loading from goods sent at invoice price

## Departmental Accounts
### Departmental accounts and allocation of expenses
= Trading and profit and loss accounts by department and the bases of apportionment.
- Departmental accounts show each department's results to judge its performance.
- Common expenses are apportioned on suitable bases: rent by floor area, power by horsepower or units, advertising by sales, wages by hours or employees.
- Inter-departmental transfers at a profit leave unrealised profit in closing stock, which is removed as a stock reserve.
> Apportion common expenses on fair bases
> Prepare departmental trading and profit and loss accounts
## Insurance Claims
### Claims for loss of stock
= Estimating stock lost by fire and computing the claim with the average clause.
- Stock on the date of fire is estimated with a memorandum trading account using the normal gross profit ratio.
- Stock lost = Estimated stock − Salvage.
- Average clause: Claim = Loss of stock × (Policy value ÷ Value of stock on the date of fire), when the stock is under-insured.
- Abnormal items (slow-moving stock) are dealt with separately at their own gross profit rate.
> Estimate stock destroyed by fire
> Compute an insurance claim under the average clause
ex: Stock on the date of fire ₹2,00,000; salvage ₹20,000; policy ₹1,50,000.
ex: Loss = 1,80,000. Claim = 1,80,000 × 1,50,000 ÷ 2,00,000 = ₹1,35,000.
## Dissolution of Partnership Firms
### Dissolution and the Garner v Murray rule
= Realisation account, insolvency of a partner and the Garner v Murray decision.
- On dissolution, assets and outside liabilities go to the realisation account; profit or loss on realisation is shared in the profit-sharing ratio.
- Order of settlement (section 48, Partnership Act, 1932): outside creditors, partners' loans, partners' capital, then any surplus in the profit-sharing ratio.
- Garner v Murray: the solvent partners bear an insolvent partner's capital deficiency in the ratio of their capitals before dissolution, after making good their share of the realisation loss in cash.
> Close the books of a dissolved firm
> Apply the Garner v Murray rule
### Piecemeal distribution
= Paying partners as assets are realised, by the proportionate capital and maximum loss methods.
- Cash is applied first to realisation expenses and outside liabilities, then to partners.
- Proportionate capital (surplus capital) method: pay first the partners whose capitals exceed their share in the profit ratio.
- Maximum loss method: treat each instalment as if no more cash will come.
> Prepare a statement of piecemeal distribution
> Compare the two methods

## Underwriting of Shares
### Underwriting and underwriting commission
= What underwriting is, its kinds, marked and unmarked applications, and the commission allowed by law.
- Underwriting is an agreement under which the underwriter undertakes to take up the shares or debentures not subscribed by the public, in return for a commission.

## Valuation of Goodwill
### Methods of valuing goodwill
= Average profit, super profit, capitalisation and annuity methods.
- Goodwill is the value of a firm's reputation and its ability to earn more than normal profits; it is an intangible asset.
- Average profit method: Goodwill = Average maintainable profit × Number of years' purchase.
- Super profit method: Normal profit = Capital employed × Normal rate of return ÷ 100; Super profit = Average maintainable profit − Normal profit; Goodwill = Super profit × Number of years' purchase.
- Capitalisation of average profit: Capitalised value = Average profit × 100 ÷ Normal rate of return; Goodwill = Capitalised value − Net assets (capital employed).
- Capitalisation of super profit: Goodwill = Super profit × 100 ÷ Normal rate of return.
- Annuity method: Goodwill = Super profit × present value of an annuity of ₹1 for the given years at the given rate.

## Introduction to Cost Accounting and the Cost Sheet
### Cost concepts and the cost sheet
= Elements of cost, classification and preparing a cost sheet.
- Elements of cost: materials, labour and expenses, each direct or indirect; indirect ones form overheads.

### Stock levels and EOQ
= Economic order quantity and the reorder, minimum and maximum stock levels.

- Maximum level = Reorder level + Reorder quantity − (Minimum consumption × Minimum reorder period).

## Labour
### Incentive plans
= Time rate, piece rate and the Halsey and Rowan premium plans.
- Time saved = Time allowed − Time taken.
- Halsey plan: Bonus = 50% × Time saved × Rate per hour; Earnings = Time taken × Rate + Bonus.

## Overheads
### Allocation, apportionment and absorption

### Marginal costing and break-even analysis
= Contribution, P/V ratio, break-even point and margin of safety.

- P/V ratio = Contribution ÷ Sales × 100.

- Margin of safety = Actual sales − Break-even sales; also Profit ÷ P/V ratio.

## Financial Markets
### Money market and capital market
= Money market instruments, the primary and secondary capital markets, and stock exchanges.
- The money market deals in short-term funds (up to one year): call money, treasury bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit, repos.
- The capital market deals in long-term funds: the primary market issues new securities (IPO, FPO, rights, private placement); the secondary market trades existing ones.
- India's main stock exchanges are the BSE (Sensex) and NSE (Nifty 50); trades settle on T+1.
- Depositories NSDL and CDSL hold securities in demat form.
